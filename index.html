The gap: retention software is built for companies with a marketing team.

Big brands track churn with tools that need a data team to set up. A six-person gym has a front desk and a spreadsheet, so lapsed customers go unnoticed until the revenue is already gone.

What exists today

Enterprise marketing platforms: powerful, priced and designed for companies with dedicated staff. Generic mass texting: cheap, but it blasts everyone the same message.

What Regulars does

Plugs into the booking or POS data a shop already has, scores each customer against their own habits, and only contacts the ones who are slipping. Set up in an afternoon, no marketing hire needed.

How a customer gets scored

A salon client at 30 days is on schedule. A gym member at 30 days is probably gone. So the score compares each customer to their own rhythm, not a fixed cutoff.

risk = 1 / (1 + e−2.2 (r − 2.2))

where r = days since last visit ÷ that customer's usual gap between visits. At r = 2.2 (more than twice their usual gap) the risk crosses 50%. Contact order is ranked by risk × monthly value, so the budget goes to the customers worth the most.

The plan

Customer
Independent gyms, cafés, salons and boutiques with 100 to 3,000 repeat customers.
Pricing
14-day free trial, then $49 a month. One recovered gym member covers the bill.
Go to market
In-person pitches to Salt Lake City businesses, starting with a free churn report built from their own data.
Stage
Working prototype. Next step: three pilot businesses and measured win-back rates.
